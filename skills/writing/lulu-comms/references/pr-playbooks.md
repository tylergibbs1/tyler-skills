# PR playbooks

The rigorous version of her PR doctrine, in one line:

> **Own the source of truth and the strategic narrative. Build direct audience access. Then use earned media selectively when its audience, credibility, format, or amplification advances the business objective.**

Hybrid owned-plus-earned, not abolition of media relations. She explicitly says agencies are useful for pitching, outreach, launches, and crises (never for story and strategy), that ignoring mainstream media is a mistake, and that traditional media retains particular value for government, financial transactions, and audiences you don't own. "The specific publication matters less than the right journalist."

**Direct by default for ownership. Hybrid by design for distribution and validation.**

## The tactics formula

> **<COMMS PLAN X> will instill <BELIEF A> in <AUDIENCE B>, making them take <ACTION C>, which achieves <BUSINESS OBJECTIVE D>.**

Solve backwards: define D first (limit to ~3), then C, then B, then A, and only then generate X. "If you can't draw a straight solid line from a given comms tactic to a desired business outcome, that's a sure sign of wasted motion."

## Audience frameworks

- **Concentric circles.** Propagate messages inside-out: yourself (for clarity) → co-founders → executives → employees → investors → power users → wider world. Substack sent updates to power users first.
- **Tribal elders.** Find the ~10 amplifiers who each credibly tell 10 more people. Win them first.
- **Cultural erogenous zones.** "It's a huge lift to change someone's worldview. It's a light lift to shape your thing to fit their worldview or passions." Build "an API to your audience."
- **The physics framework.** "Communication is a vector, not a scalar": it only matters with a direction attached. Under attack? Spread the force (pressure = force / surface area). Attacking? Concentrate on a specific point.

## Announcements and launches

**The 5 ingredients** (from her Ramp fundraising case study): (1) objectives, (2) audience, (3) message, (4) execution, (5) validation.

- "The general public doesn't matter. Your audience consists of the people who will decide your fate" (usually employees/recruits and customers).
- The biggest mistake is making it about the money: "'We are excited to announce that we have more money now, thanks for the money, we like it.' That's boring and self-centered." Put customers at the center.
- Founders share the news in their own voices; the most impactful validators are customers, employees, and investors.
- Address a potentially negative fact directly before critics frame it (Ramp addressed its context head-on and still worked with Bloomberg after a leak; going direct doesn't mean icing out press).

**Launch cheat sheet:** goal is hiring → highlight team, mission, funding. Goal is sales → highlight product benefits and features. Goal is fundraising → highlight founders and market need. Messaging always reflects the goal; never mix.

**Launch announcement template**

> **Today we're announcing [news].**
>
> The important part is what it means for **[customer/audience]**: **[benefit]**.
>
> Over the past **[period]**, we've **[specific evidence/result]**. **[Customer or independent validation]**.
>
> One thing deserves context: **[potentially negative or misunderstood fact]**. Here is what happened and why: **[plain explanation]**.
>
> Next, we're using **[capital/resource/news]** to **[specific plan]**.
>
> **[CTA for customers / candidates / partners].**

**Targeted media pitch template**

Subject: `[timely development]: what it means for [reporter's specific beat]`

> Hi [Name],
>
> You've been covering **[specific topic]**. **[One-sentence news hook]**.
>
> **[Founder/expert]** can show you **[specific evidence/demo/data]** and explain why **[counterintuitive or consequential angle]**.
>
> Two useful facts: **[proof point]**; **[proof point]**.
>
> We can share **[data/demo/customer/source material]** and make **[person]** available **[availability]**.
>
> Primary material: **[link]**.

Why it works in her framework: audience fit first, immediate news hook, evidence instead of adjectives, a real human expert, and the journalist gets something substantively useful.

**Channel selection.** Evaluate: is the audience concentrated there, does the format suit the story's complexity, is the interviewer appropriate, does the appearance support the positioning? (Substack chose Joe Rogan for format fit, not just reach. A deeply relevant niche podcast beats mass-market TV; one excellent article beats ten mediocre ones.)

## Crisis comms

**"Don't get ready, stay ready."** When crisis hits, it's too late to craft a good plan. Prepare in advance:

- War room: roles, the final decision-maker, spokespersons, legal responsibilities, escalation rules, criteria for "breaking the glass."
- Offline contact list, monitoring for media and social channels, prebuilt holding statements and Q&A, scenario plans, an hour-1 checklist.

In the crisis: establish confirmed facts first, communicate with employees early where appropriate, acknowledge human impact, say what is known and unknown, correct material falsehoods fast, give scheduled updates, and never speculate. On lawyers: "your lawyers will tell you to stay quiet until the investigation is complete... letting trust bleed out is way worse than potentially sharing too much." (Weigh this against her own playbook, which keeps legal, compliance, and regulators inside the response structure; the point is deliberate tradeoff, not ignoring counsel.)

**Crisis holding statement template**

> At **[time/date]**, we learned **[confirmed fact]**.
>
> This has affected **[people/customers]** by **[specific known impact]**, and our immediate priority is **[human/operational priority]**.
>
> We have **[actions already taken]**.
>
> We do not yet know **[important uncertainty]**, and we will not speculate.
>
> Our next update will be **[specific time/channel]**.

## The apology

Apologize when wrong, never when right. Five ingredients:

1. **Say the actual words.** "I'm sorry," from a specific named human. "I'm sorry I…" never "I'm sorry if…"
2. **Be specific about what you did wrong.** No euphemisms, no passive voice ("mistakes were made").
3. **"Berate yourself so they don't have to."** (Ben Horowitz: "if you are going to eat shit, don't nibble.")
4. **Make amends**, but only promise what's "airtight and true."
5. **Follow through, then follow up, unprompted.**

**Apology template**

> I'm sorry. We **[specific thing done wrong]**.
>
> That caused **[specific impact]**, and we are responsible for **[scope of responsibility]**.
>
> Here is what happened: **[brief explanation, not disguised as an excuse]**.
>
> We have already **[repair]**. By **[date]**, we will **[commitment you are certain you can meet]**.
>
> I will update you again on **[date/time]**.

## Defense: the hard target

Being a soft target invites attacks: "attacking you becomes a lottery ticket." Admit fault when wrong; defend yourself when right. "Resistance is escalation," and "tit for two tats": forgive once, not twice. Anduril rule #8, turn FUD to fuel: "Every time they attack you, it should be unpleasant for them... It should create the opposite of job security for them."

Triage first: is the attack material, and does it reach people who matter? If yes, respond fast and forcefully. If no, ignore it: "nobody gives a shit about you... if you flop, people will just move on."

**When the claim is false, fight the story with a better story, not a fact dump.** Correct the central false claim, provide simple primary-source evidence, then supply a coherent, more accurate narrative people can remember. "If you're fighting a story with a statistic, you're losing." Communicate to the actual audience, not to the journalist as the ultimate audience. Humor helps only when tone and facts support it.

**False-claim response template**

> **One factual point needs correcting: [claim] is not true.**
>
> Here is the evidence: **[primary-source evidence]**.
>
> What actually happened was **[short, chronologically intelligible story]**.
>
> The complete record is here: **[owned source]**.

## The Anduril 10 rules

Her fullest campaign playbook ("from black sheep to household name"): (1) start with the ends (ends/ways/means); (2) go direct; (3) nail the narrative and repeat it relentlessly; (4) act like an insurgent, not an incumbent; (5) increase pressure by decreasing surface area; (6) inner circle comes first; (7) win over your tribal elders; (8) turn FUD to fuel; (9) take more risk ("you have to say things to stand out"); (10) stay the course, "there is no peacetime." Focus only on the persuadable middle; ignore those who'd never come around.

Execution bias throughout: "A good comms plan, violently executed now, is better than a perfect comms plan that languishes in Google Docs."
